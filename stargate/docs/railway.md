# Stargate: Train Station Mode

Maintainer reference for trains through the gate. Player-facing behaviour is in the [README](../README.md). Code: `scripts/railway.lua`, `prototypes/rails.lua`, and the station parts of `scripts/gate.lua` and `scripts/gui.lua`. The carriage-by-carriage clone technique follows RiftRail; the code is this mod's own. See [Compared with RiftRail](#compared-with-riftrail) and [Provenance](#provenance).

The rule throughout: a train behaves like a vanilla train at a vanilla signal until the gate takes it, and from then on the gate finishes what it started.

## Layout

The gate never rotates, so lanes are always north-south: trains enter heading north and leave the partner heading south. Station mode is `record.railway`: `single` (one central track used both ways), `right`, or `left` (two tracks, departures on the right or left of a train driving in); `LAYOUTS` gives each lane's x offset. `build()` places, and adds whatever is missing after a configuration change or an older layout:

- `bte-stargate-rail` pieces, centres 2, 4, and 6 tiles south of the gate's centre, from its south edge to a tile behind the ring's plane, and `bte-stargate-rail-hidden` on behind the ring to 5 tiles north of the centre, long enough for a carriage to pass wholly behind the veil and for arrivals to appear there. Both are vanilla straight rails on the `rail` collision layer alone, so they fit the gate's mask while player rails still cannot be built over them and join them at the edge.
- Vanilla rail layers are all below `floor`, where the gate's concrete is drawn, so the rails are drawn on `zero`, under the ground, and the gate draws its own track: the `bte-stargate-tracks` companion on `floor-mechanics`, from the model's `rails-single`/`rails-double` layers (a riveted steel deck, ties and rails at the game's sizes and gauge, on a ramp over the ring's lower arc), the two-track pictures adding `chevrons-right` or `chevrons-left` (`TRACK_PICTURES` in `scripts/gate.lua`). `show_body` gives the body the matching `main-<mode>` and `glow-lights[-red]-<mode>` variation, with the track cut out of the building and its lamps hidden behind it.
- The hidden `bte-stargate-station` train stop beside the departure lane at the far end of the hidden rails, behind the ring, named after the gate and with no collision box. Trains brake to stop there, 1.4 tiles short of the rails' end, so they still cross the edge, where they are taken, at speed: an automatic train from 370 tiles out crossed it at 0.29 tiles a tick (measured). At the edge it would have stopped with its front 0.6 inside, crawling up to it. The gate window opens that stop's own window instead (`player.opened = station` works although the stop is not selectable) and attaches itself to its right as a relative GUI anchored to `train_stop_gui` (`scripts/gui.lua`).
- The signals, below.

`Gate.remove_railway` removes the rails, the station, and the signals, and clears carriages off the rails. The mode cannot change while a carriage is on the gate's rails or a transfer runs either way.

`record.rail_layout` marks the layout version (`LAYOUT`), and `Railway.check` upgrades older gates there rather than in `on_configuration_changed`, because a reinstall with the same mod version raises none: a gate built before the hidden rails left trains grinding against the old rail end in the ring.

## Signals

- `bte-stargate-exit-signal`, a rail signal on the arrival lane at the edge, west of it facing north: the only side and direction a signal guarding southbound trains attaches (measured; any other side or direction is placed but never attached). Drawn on `zero` with its lights at zero intensity.
- `bte-stargate-entry-signal` on the departure lane, east of it facing south, for trains heading in. It keeps the vanilla pictures, so players see whether the gate is ready: red while it cannot send, green, then yellow as a train reserves its way in. On a single track it also keeps the lane two-way for the pathfinder, which a signal on one side only would make one-way.
- The entry signal is switched by `bte-stargate-entry-control`, a hidden constant combinator on the same spot, wired to it with a script wire (`defines.wire_origin.script`): the signal closes while the combinator sends `signal-red` (`close_signal` with `signal-red > 0`). A signal ignores its circuit condition without a network, so the script cannot close it alone (measured).
- Both are unselectable and sit on the `rail` collision layer alone: signals must collide with signals, and the player cannot build another on the same spot.

`Railway.check` (every 3 ticks) works out for each station gate whether a train could go through now: the partner is a station not receiving a transfer (`linked`), the gate is open, and the partner's exit signal is `open` (its first block free and not reserved). It switches the entry signal only when that changes (`record.entry_open`). Room behind the partner's veil is not checked: an entity search per gate every check was three quarters of the sync's cost, and a free exit with no transfer running nearly always means room. Measured on a save with three station gates: about 2.4 µs per gate check (profilers included); the game draws the signal's state itself, so showing it costs nothing more.

## Taking a train

The signal decides for automatic trains; the edge only checks that the partner can receive. While a train is in the gate's block (`trains_in_block` of `record.edge_rail`, a departure-lane piece inside the entry signal; the edge piece is cut by it), the check looks for one whose front has reached the edge, moving in or stopped, not just arrived (below) and not already passing through:

- If the partner is `linked`, it is taken when it is automatic (or held, below), or the gate is open. An automatic train is in the block only because the signal let it in, or because it had reserved its way through before the signal closed: a vanilla train keeps a reservation when a signal closes by circuit, and kept going in the test, which closes the gate with the train 30 tiles out. So no state records the signal's promise. A train driven by hand ignores the signal, as at any signal, and goes while the gate is open, the crash rules covering a blocked exit.
- If not, an automatic train is held (`hold()`: manual, speed 0, in `storage.rail_held`) at once, from whatever speed, or it would drive on into the ring towards the station behind it, and taken when the partner can receive; it arrives in automatic mode. A held train goes back to automatic if its gate goes or leaves station mode, or the train is moved off the lane. A train driven by hand into a closed gate is not taken and rolls on to the rails' end.

No train looks for a path to its next stop, on the other surface, before it is taken, so the game never raises the "no path" alert: waiting trains stand at the red signal still heading for the gate's station, and a train let in is taken at the edge, long before it reaches the station (it would raise the alert once it left the station, seen in a visible game).

## Sending

1. Once taken (above), `Railway.update` (every tick) holds the train in manual mode and drives both parts at one speed: the speed the train came in with, gaining speed eased in: none until the first carriage is through, then half what a vanilla train would, rising to the full rate over the next carriage's length (`transfer.ease`, `EASE_LENGTH`; gaining at once, right after braking for the station, looked jerky in game). While carriages are still going in, the part already out, which drives itself, is held to that speed (`keep_speed()`): its lone front locomotive gained speed several times faster, and the rest keeping up with it jerked the train forward (seen in game); once all are through, or the part out is handed on, the rest keeps up with it instead. A carriage past the ring waiting for room behind the partner's veil rolls on for up to `SLACK` (1 tile) rather than stopping, which stood the train still for a tick at each carriage. The full rate is `ACCELERATION * (locomotives * fuel acceleration multiplier) / weight` (measured from a standstill: 0.0011 tiles a tick each tick for one locomotive and four wagons on coal, the same for two and eight, three times that on nuclear fuel), up to the train's top speed; air resistance is left out. A train with no working locomotive is driven at `SPEED` (0.3, 65 km/h) at least. The splash timings scale with the speed. Each carriage whose rear has passed the ring's plane is disconnected, `rotate()`d (the clone keeps the orientation, and must leave facing the way it entered relative to its motion), `clone`d onto the partner's arrival lane, and destroyed. `transfer.car` follows the leading carriage, so no tick walks every carriage. A carriage that cannot be turned or cloned is put back as it was and tried again next tick; after `STUCK_TICKS` failures it is wrecked like one with no room, since a started train must finish. (`clone` ignores collisions, so an obstacle that is not rolling stock does not make it fail: a chest on the arrival lane did not.)
2. The first clone appears behind the partner's veil, a locomotive's front at the hidden rails' end, and each next one exactly one coupling length, `(joint_distance + connection_distance) / 2` per carriage (7 for vanilla), behind the one before, once that has moved at least so far: at speed it has moved up to a tick's travel further, and a clone at the rail end would then not couple. They auto-couple as they are placed; a three-carriage train arriving at 1.3 tiles a tick did. No leading locomotive is needed: setting `speed` on a manual train works without locomotives or fuel (a lone cargo wagon kept the speed it was given), so the arriving part is simply driven out every tick.
3. Drivers are moved by hand, since `clone` does not copy them; a remote driver is put out as for vehicles. Players looking at a carriage's window are moved to the clone's.
4. Each carriage plays `bte-stargate-splash` (the model's `glow-splash`, at the lane's x offset), a short cyan light, and the `stargate-car-in`/`-out` sounds on both gates (`splash()`), timed so the flash peaks as it vanishes and appears.
5. The arriving part (`move_out()`, every tick until the transfer ends) is in one of three modes:
   - **Driven by script** while it has no schedule back: a train driven by hand, or one whose first locomotive facing out has not arrived yet (wagons first, or pushed in backwards). It goes straight out at the transfer speed. A train driven by hand gets its schedule and mode back when the transfer ends and leaves at that speed.
   - **Driving itself**, from when a locomotive facing out arrives (`steer()`): the train gets back its schedule, interrupts, group, and temporary stops (`rail` records name places on the old surface and are dropped; the gate's own stop is skipped, or removed if temporary), `transfer.stop` keeps its current stop, and it is set to automatic at the transfer speed, since restoring the schedule leaves a fresh clone standing (the carriages behind then waited at the ring for it and the whole train crawled, seen in game). The game now picks its branches: a train pushed straight out missed a fork's branch (seen in game). Each coupling makes the train manual, sets its schedule back to the first record, and slows it for the added weight (measured), so `update()` gives back the speed it had and `drive_itself()` sets its stop and automatic mode again. The game still reports the manual state on that tick, so the state is not checked then (a false push in a save replay).
   - **Pushed** (`transfer.pushing`), from the first tick it would slow down: its speed drops below the previous tick's (`transfer.pace`), or its state is anything but `on_the_path` (braking for a signal or station, no path). It goes straight on in manual mode, at the speed it had, gathering speed with the departing part, `SPEED` at least, until the transfer ends; it is then handed back automatic. Left to brake, a train stopped with carriages in the ring and the crash rules crushed its front, or every carriage when it had no path on a broken track (seen in game); handing it back after each carriage let it stop again before the next was out. Manual, because a speed set on an automatic train that wants to stop is ignored (measured). Pushed, it may miss a branch or run past a red signal; the rails' end or what it runs into stops it, and then the crash rules apply.

   The departing part is driven at the transfer speed, raised each tick to the arrived part's when that drives itself faster: otherwise the next clone appeared at the rail end already falling behind and did not couple, and the train's rear came out loose (a seven-carriage train in a save, the arrived part at 0.69 tiles a tick, the rest fed at 0.51). A carriage waits at the ring's plane until the one before has moved on far enough.

One transfer per arrival lane at a time (`receiving`). On a single track, arrivals start on the departure lane, so only trains moving in or stopped are taken (judged by the end on the departure lane, `in_sign()`: a train coming round a curve can have its tail further north than its head, and comparing the two ends' `y` read it as driving out, so it was taken only after braking almost to a stop at the station; a save, and a test), never the part of a transfer still coming out (`arrival()`) except when chained (below), and an automatic train only when it is heading for the gate's station or standing at it (`bound_for()`), not one that stopped there on its way elsewhere. A train driven by hand that came out of a gate is not taken by that gate for 300 ticks (`storage.rail_guard`, per carriage and gate). A time guard on every arrival, at every gate, kept a train that came straight back into a gate standing at its ring with no path until it ran out (seen in game). Handing an arrived train its schedule sets its speed again (`keep_speed()`), which the game refuses with an error when its next stop lies behind it, a locomotive at its rear to take it back; it then brakes and turns by itself.

A train long enough to reach another station gate while its tail is still coming out of the first is chained (`hand_over()`, from `Railway.check` of the gate its head reaches): that gate takes it when it would take the same train arriving on its own: automatic, with that gate's station its next stop (read from the schedule, since the first transfer may be pushing it manually; a train with no locomotive facing out ahead, wagons first, is handed its schedule there and then, as `steer()` would), or driven by hand while the gate is open; never the gate it is still coming out of. Otherwise it jams: the first transfer counts as blocked (`transfer.jammed`) and wrecks what is still coming out, as at a blocked exit, and ends, leaving the train standing in the next gate's entrance. Taken, the second transfer drives it, and the first (`handed`) stops moving, steering, or crushing it and only adds carriages at its tail, at the second's speed, until its own sender is empty; the second keeps sending them as they come. Chains of any length repeat this pairwise (a test runs three at once). The second's saved current record is the first's stop, since each coupling set the schedule back to the first record. On a loop the train's tail can be further south than its head, so the end going in is the one on the gate's departure lane (`head()`, `drive_in()`) and the push out is away from the tail at the arrival lane (`out_sign()`, `drive_out()`), not by comparing the two ends' `y` as the straight lanes otherwise allow. Without this, a train the next gate did not take was pushed against the end of its hidden rails for ever, its tail waiting at the sender (a test).

### Drawing glitch at the first send

When the locomotive goes in, what is left becomes a new train. If Factorio makes that train's far end its front (`front_stock`; its speed turns negative), the train is drawn shifted along the track from that tick on, though every carriage's position moves on smoothly: in a player's save a long train of fluid wagons on a loop was drawn two tiles ahead at once, and in the gate lab one stood still for a frame. Later sends take carriages from the other end, so the front does not change again and nothing more shows.

Whether the front flips depends only on the approach: a train that came south and round a U-turn to the east flips, one turning west or coming straight does not; the station mode does not matter. Found with the method in [testing.md](testing.md#seeing-what-is-drawn). Nothing tried at the send changed it: no uncoupling or turning, a fresh carriage instead of `clone`, leaving the rest's speed alone, even sending by `destroy()` alone. A vanilla train split the same way, without this mod, is shifted too, so it is the engine's drawing, and no API chooses a train's front. Left as is.

## Crashes

Once started, the whole train is sent, whatever the signals, the gate's power, or its state do. When the arrived part has stood still for `STUCK_TICKS` and a carriage finds no room (`crash()`):

- first every arrived carriage not yet out of the ring is crushed (`crush()`): its rear more than `EMERGED_SLACK` behind the ring's plane (a locomotive stopped at the edge with no track beyond has its rear 0.2 tiles behind, and survives);
- then the carriage itself, against the rear of the carriage ahead.

After the last carriage is sent, the transfer runs on until that carriage is out, or crushes it if the train stops first. `wreck()` destroys a carriage with its cargo, places its explosion and remnants at the partner, and puts its driver on the floor beside the arrival lane towards the gate's centre: between the tracks of a two-track gate, east of a single one, clear of the track and in front of the portal.

If either gate is removed during a transfer (`Railway.gate_removed`), carriages caught on the removed gate's own rails are wrecked with their drivers thrown clear (since rails cannot be removed from under rolling stock), while connected carriages outside the sender stay there with their schedule and mode restored and those already past the partner keep their copy. Expired `storage.rail_guard` entries are pruned as transfers finish.

## Known limits

- Wait conditions at the gate's station do not delay a train: the gate takes it as soon as it reaches the edge. The station is a destination for schedules, not a place to wait.
- Two trains waiting at each other's single-track gates wait for ever: each stands in the other's exit block. As with a vanilla single track without a passing loop, give such routes two tracks.
- A gate has one partner, and trains always go there; there is no routing between several gates.
- A train driven by hand into a closed gate rolls on behind the veil to the rails' end, half hidden, until the gate opens.
- A transfer costs no power beyond keeping the gate open, and an automatic train let in or a started train goes even if the gate closes.
- A train held at the edge stops at once, from whatever speed. It happens only when the partner stops being a station, or another train is still arriving there, while a train is in the gate's block. If the gate stays a station with no partner, the train waits there in manual mode, with no alert, until the gate is paired again or the train is moved.
- An arriving train that would slow down before all of it is through is pushed straight on, past signals and forks, so it can miss its branch, run past a red signal, or run into the train ahead; it then finds a new path from where it ends up. A train driven by hand, or one whose first locomotive facing out has not come through yet, is pushed straight out the same way. Interrupts can see a train that is only partly through.
- Untested: a train limit on the gate's station; LTN and Cybersyn; whether `clone` keeps quality and spoilage (assumed); the look of trains at the ring beyond the send glitch above.

## Compared with RiftRail

Both move a train carriage by carriage with the same engine calls: disconnect, `rotate()`, `clone()` onto the other surface, move the driver by hand (`clone` does not copy one), destroy the original. They are the only way to move a loaded carriage across surfaces, so any mod doing this uses them; Space Exploration's elevator clones carriages too. That technique, and the engine behaviour it relies on, was studied from RiftRail's source. No RiftRail code is copied: the code around the technique is built for the gate, and everything else follows the gate's own model, closer to vanilla rail behaviour.

| | RiftRail | Stargate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moving a carriage | disconnect, rotate when the two portals' directions call for it (portals can face any way), clone, move the driver, destroy | the same engine calls, for fixed north-south lanes: every carriage is turned; one that cannot be turned or cloned is retried, then wrecked |
| Spacing at the exit | geometry from the portals' directions | each clone placed one coupling length behind the last, from the carriages' prototypes |
| Trigger | a train destroys a 1-HP collider on the portal track | the 3-tick check finds a train's front at the gate's edge; no collider, no damage events |
| Waiting | the train stops at the portal station; integrations add a circuit wait condition that keeps it there | the train waits in front of a script-controlled vanilla signal, automatic and still heading for the station; it is held at the edge only when the partner cannot receive |
| Blocked exit | the transfer stalls, the carriages waiting on both sides | the exit block is checked before a train is let past the entry signal; after that, carriages that cannot come out are destroyed |
| Arriving train | automatic from the first carriage, a helper locomotive leading when that is not a locomotive facing out; pushed at a set speed | drives itself once a locomotive facing out has arrived, and is pushed straight on only if it would slow down before all of it is through; driven by script before that; no helper locomotive |
| Schedule | copied with the first carriage; temporary "fake interrupt" stops are stripped afterwards | copied once, when the first locomotive facing out arrives, then only its current stop set again after each coupling; the train's own temporary stops are kept |
| Routing | Entry, Exit, and Standby portals; several exits per entry, chosen by a circuit signal | one partner per gate, both ways; one or two tracks |
| Integrations | LTN, Cybersyn 2, Space Exploration | none yet |
| Other travel | players can teleport from the portal's core | the same gate carries players on foot, vehicles, and enemies ([transfer.md](transfer.md)) |
