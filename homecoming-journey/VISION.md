# Beyond the Edge: Homecoming Journey — vision

**Fifteen ancient gates. Five lost artifacts. One way home.**

Homecoming Journey is the campaign that brings the Beyond the Edge family together. It gives an established factory a new purpose: uncover a forgotten interplanetary network, restore its machinery, and use what lies beyond it to bring the engineer safely home.

The journey begins with an unexplained signal on Nauvis. It ends with a massive gate carried to the far end of the Shattered Planet route. Between those moments, the player fights for remote footholds, deciphers an ancient technology, and turns scattered ruins into a network connecting planetary factories.

> This is a planned campaign, not implemented gameplay. Power figures are campaign design targets. The implementation roadmap and candidate balance live in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md). Standalone combat belongs to [Mechanoids](../mechanoids/VISION.md), and standalone gate construction and transport belong to [Stargate](../stargate/VISION.md).

## The next chapter of Beyond the Edge

Homecoming Journey has hard requirements on all four other family mods:

- [Space Lab](../spacelab/README.md) makes self-sufficient orbital industry possible.
- [Warp Drive](../warp-drive/README.md) turns deep-space exploration into research and faster travel.
- [Mechanoids — Guardians of the Ruins](../mechanoids/VISION.md) supplies the ancient mechanical defenders and encounter behavior.
- [Stargate](../stargate/VISION.md) supplies gate construction, player transfer, pairing, and train logistics.

The campaign gives these capabilities a destination and a personal reason to keep building: learn to live in space, venture beyond familiar routes, and discover a way home. Unlike the other four mods, Homecoming Journey is not standalone. Installing Mechanoids and Stargate together without this campaign does not silently enable its story or restrictions.

## The journey

### 1. A signal on Nauvis

The Warp Drive's first successful activation detects something unexpected: unusual **tachyon activity on Nauvis**.

The signal points to a distant, unexplored part of the map. Something has been there all along, far beyond the factory's familiar boundaries.

Reaching the coordinates reveals the remains of an enormous ring-shaped structure. Biters occupy the surrounding ruins, while ancient **Mechanoids** still defend the installation. These machines belong to the gate network's forgotten security system, and they recognize the engineer as an intruder.

The first objective is straightforward: prepare an expedition and clear the site. Advanced weapons, armor, ammunition, and supplies should matter. The player has a powerful factory behind them; this is a reason to use it.

### 2. Understand what was left behind

Once the defenders are cleared, the ruin becomes accessible. Its surfaces and control mechanisms carry unfamiliar runes, encrypted records, and traces of a technology the engineer cannot yet reproduce.

Inspecting them reveals a new research opportunity: **Ruin Deciphering**.

The factory now has a question to answer. With Warp Drive installed, its tachyon-assisted research can help the laboratories make sense of the discovery. The same phenomenon that exposed the ruin becomes a means of understanding it.

Research turns an archaeological curiosity into a practical engineering project. The player learns how to restore the gate by delivering tungsten, electronic components, and other advanced materials directly to the installation.

As restoration completes, the ruined structure becomes a functional Stargate. Bringing it online requires **1 GW of power**, with **500 MW needed to maintain its active field**. Establishing power at a remote site is part of claiming it.

The reward is tangible: a piece of ancient machinery has become a working part of the player's infrastructure.

### 3. Step through the first gate

The first gate opens a connection to **Gleba**.

Stepping through takes the engineer to another distant, unexplored location, far from an established landing site or factory. There is another ruined Stargate here, surrounded by pentapods and Mechanoid defenders.

The player recognizes the opportunity, but the environment changes the expedition. Gleba demands different preparation, a secure foothold, and reliable supplies. The ruin must be cleared, understood, repaired, and powered before it becomes an ordinary working connection.

An expedition should also leave room to retreat and resupply. First contact with an unrestored gate needs a recoverable return route; an unexpected shortage must not leave the campaign permanently stranded.

After restoring the Gleba gate and using it to return, the network responds. Another tachyon signal appears, revealing a ruin on **Vulcanus**. Restoring one connection has exposed evidence of another.

This establishes the campaign's rhythm:

**Discover → secure → decipher → restore → connect → explore further.**

### 4. Rediscover an interplanetary network

The complete network contains **three Stargates on each of the five existing planets: fifteen gates in total**.

Every world gives the same underlying expedition a different character:

| Planet | The expedition's character |
| --- | --- |
| **Nauvis** | First contact: hostile wildlife, mechanical defenders, and the discovery that the ruins can be understood and restored. |
| **Gleba** | A difficult biological frontier, where pentapods and Mechanoids threaten a remote foothold. |
| **Vulcanus** | Heavily guarded mechanical installations amid lava and demolisher territory. The organized defenders are Mechanoids; demolishers remain a danger of the surrounding world. |
| **Fulgora** | Ruins on constrained islands, where lightning and electrical infrastructure shape the expedition. |
| **Aquilo** | A remote, frozen destination that demands heating, supplies, and a dependable route back through the network. |

The ancient gates form a network of connections the player must discover and learn to use. Knowing that a destination exists does not mean every ancient gate can reach it directly. **There is no direct Nauvis–Aquilo connection in the campaign network: reaching Aquilo through it requires at least one intermediate hop.** How player-built experimental gates coexist with this progression remains an explicit campaign design decision; this is not a restriction imposed by standalone Stargate.

That makes each restored connection valuable. A gate can provide a new foothold, a more convenient route, access to another expedition, or a place worth defending and keeping powered. The player is rebuilding a transport network with meaningful geography.

Some sites reveal further addresses or encrypted clues. One site on each planet holds something more significant: a unique artifact.

### 5. Find the five missing pieces

The artifacts are surviving components of a much larger machine. Each planet contributes one distinct piece, for **five unique artifacts** across the campaign.

Recovering the **first artifact unlocks research into the Massive Stargate**. This gives the player a larger goal while much of the network is still unexplored. They can begin understanding the design and preparing the industrial effort while searching for the remaining pieces.

Completing the new gate requires **all five artifacts**, incorporated into its construction alongside substantial quantities of high-tech materials. Ordinary production can supply the frame, machinery, and supporting systems; the ancient components must be recovered through exploration.

The encrypted records found along the way help explain how the pieces fit together and how the completed gate can be brought online. Research, exploration, and construction converge on the same objective.

### 6. Train Stargate transport

Personal travel establishes the network. **Train transport turns it into an industrial system**, connecting the factories that will supply the enormous research and construction effort ahead.

With further research and rail upgrades, a restored gate can become part of a planetary railway. Trains carrying bulk materials, advanced components, and science packs pass through to another planet and continue their delivery routes. A remote ruin can grow into a major logistics terminal, with stations, buffers, power generation, and a steady flow of cargo.

The player can organize production around each world's strengths: bring Vulcanus metals into manufacturing centers, distribute Fulgora electronics, and gather planetary science into a sustained research supply chain. Faster, more direct deliveries can also help perishable goods reach their destination in useful condition. Planetary rail networks feed the launch infrastructure that continues supplying the orbital project.

The final research is intended to be an enormous factory-scale undertaking. Gate trains give the player a way to increase throughput and remove transport bottlenecks as that demand grows. Discovering the artifacts opens the opportunity; optimizing the factory makes completing the work achievable.

That creates new engineering decisions:

- Which gates should become high-throughput hubs, and which are best kept for expeditions?
- Where should production, research, and cargo buffers sit relative to the available connections?
- Can the receiving stations and power grids sustain the traffic, or does the bottleneck simply move to the other end?

Trains follow the discovered gate connections, including intermediate hops on routes to Aquilo. Building a useful service means planning the entire route, not selecting an arbitrary destination. Sustained bulk traffic gives the gates' substantial operating power a productive role in the factory.

**Train transport is supplied by Stargate and is part of the full Homecoming Journey vision.** The campaign adds the enormous research demand and ancient network; it does not implement a second train-transfer system. The player can combine gate trains with conventional Space Age transport to support the journey home.

### 7. Build a gate in space

The Massive Stargate has one extraordinary restriction: **it can only be built on a space platform**.

The player must plan a platform around it, deliver the artifacts and construction materials, and provide room for the power, propulsion, defenses, and supplies that such a structure requires. The project draws on the whole factory and the planetary network built during the campaign.

Completing the structure brings the final revelation. To open the way home, it needs **10 GW of power** and must be activated **at the far end of the Shattered Planet route**.

Building the gate was only part of the challenge. Now the player has to carry it into the deepest reaches of space and bring it online there. The final expedition tests the platform as a complete machine: can it reach the destination, survive the journey, and deliver the required power when it matters?

Warp Drive makes the voyage more achievable, Space Lab sustains orbital industry, and Stargate connects the planetary factories behind it. All are required parts of this campaign installation; no standalone campaign fallback is planned.

### 8. Go home

At the end of the route, the Massive Stargate finally activates.

The connection leads to the engineer's original home. The engineer passes through and returns safely. **This is the campaign's ending.**

The factory has accomplished something beyond producing more science or expanding farther across the map. Everything built along the way—the laboratories, expeditions, restored gates, interplanetary train services, planetary outposts, and final platform—has made this return possible.

A standard option to continue playing the existing factory can remain available after the ending, while the homecoming gives the campaign a definite conclusion.

## Battles that leave progress behind

Mechanoids should feel like recognizable security machines with distinct jobs. Fast **Skirmishers** pressure the player's movement, ranged **Sentinels** defend approaches, and heavier **Bulwarks** can anchor later encounters. Their silhouettes and attacks should help the player understand a fight and prepare for it.

Each site's organized defenders are finite. Clearing them is lasting progress: returning to a ruin, repairing a gate, or loading a save does not reset its guardian encounter. The surrounding planet can still be dangerous, and native creatures may fight the Mechanoids as well as the engineer.

The player should be able to solve difficult encounters with the tools Factorio has taught them to build—better equipment, ammunition, fortifications, artillery, support vehicles, and cooperation. Preparation and infrastructure are part of the adventure.

Fifteen sites should offer different layouts, threats, and decisions. Their purpose is to make the network feel discovered and earned, with room for both hard-fought victories and clever engineering solutions.

## A network that can be damaged, but not lost forever

The ancient ruins themselves are **indestructible**. A demolisher on Vulcanus must not be able to erase a required piece of the campaign before the player reaches it.

A restored Stargate can be damaged badly enough to collapse back into a ruin. The player then has a repair problem, while the location, discoveries, and cleared encounter remain part of their progress.

The same principle applies to artifacts and expeditions: setbacks should cost effort and create new decisions, while leaving a way to recover. The player is restoring a resilient ancient network, not gambling the entire campaign on one fragile object.

## What the campaign should feel like

- **Curiosity with a destination.** Each discovery answers one question and reveals another, gradually pointing toward the way home.
- **Danger worth preparing for.** Remote expeditions reward advanced equipment and a factory capable of supporting it.
- **Knowledge becoming infrastructure.** Deciphering a ruin changes what the player can build and where they can travel.
- **Exploration becoming industrial scale.** Train connections turn hard-won footholds into a supply network for the final research and construction effort.
- **Progress that stays earned.** Defeated guardians stay defeated, recovered knowledge remains available, and damaged gates can be restored.
- **A reason to use the endgame factory.** Power, research, logistics, and platform engineering all contribute to the final expedition.
- **A satisfying conclusion.** The last gate completes the journey with a safe return home.

## Build on independently playable mods

Stargate first establishes reliable player travel and its own experimental-to-paired progression. Mechanoids first establishes readable battles around ruins, provisionally on Fulgora. Each has a reason to exist without the campaign or the other.

Homecoming Journey then places authored encounters and ancient gates across the five planets, connects discoveries to artifacts and research, and adds the orbital homecoming. It owns this story and its progression, while the underlying mods remain the owners of combat and transport. Together, they fulfill the larger vision: discover the network, restore it, put it to work, and use it to find the way home.
